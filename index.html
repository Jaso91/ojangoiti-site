About this place

Restored with care, rooted in its history

A traditional farmhouse built in 1800, set beside a stream and the ruins of its old mill. Recently restored, surrounded by nature, quiet, and wildlife.

A place to disconnect, breathe clean air, and enjoy a stay that feels genuinely unique grounds open for children and pets, and warm, modern comfort indoors.

Surrounded by forests, rivers, and abundant wildlife, this is a place where mornings begin with birdsong and evenings end beneath a sky full of stars. Whether you're looking for peaceful walks, quality time with family, or simply a break from everyday life, you'll find the perfect setting here.

The spacious outdoor areas invite children to play freely and pets to explore, while inside you'll discover warm, welcoming spaces with original wooden beams, floors, and staircase that reflect the history and character of this special home.

The history

The mill that gave the house its name

Beside the house, along the stream, stand the ruins of the old mill that once belonged to this land.

It no longer operates, but its stone walls remain a quiet trace of the property’s past, still visible from the garden.

It stood on the bank of the Ereño (Errekalde) stream, beside the farmhouse of the same name. Its first recorded owner appears in the 1745 tax census, and it’s mentioned again in the 1810 territorial survey it ceased operating in the mid 20th century.

What remains today are traces of the dam, the millrace, the head pond, and the mill building itself. Gautegiz Arteaga had no ironworks of its own, though foundry remains have been found nearby.

Old records write the name as “Oxangoitia” the historic spelling of what’s now “Ojangoiti.” Spanish orthography merged that old “x” sound into “j” sometime between the 19th and 20th centuries, and Basque spelling itself wasn’t standardized until the 1970s likely why the name shifted along with it.
